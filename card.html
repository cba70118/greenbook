Sedgefield Country Club · Greensboro, North Carolina

A short Donald Ross course decided on the greens. The measured grades say putting is the hardest thing to do here and approach play is the easiest — 55% of approach shots come from 100–175 yards and the field hits 73% of greens, so iron play barely separates anyone. Almost every model prices this course on ball-striking. This card is built on Bermuda putting instead.

Reading this honestly

It is one bet, eight ways

All eight tickets across six players are strong Bermuda putters. Two players are doubled, so this is really about five outcomes, not eight. If the read on this course is wrong, most of the card fails together. A losing week here means the idea was wrong — not that eight separate bets ran badly.

One position is a known negative

Blades Brown at +240 carries the biggest stake and the model dislikes it: the price needs a 29% chance and the model gives 15%. He has the best scrambling in the field but no Bermuda record to check it against, and a coin-flip chance of making the cut. It is on the card as a judgement call, and logged as one.

The market moved before we finished

Two tickets were priced better when the week’s work was done than when they were placed. Cole’s top-10 went +850 to +650, and Hughes’s went +1300 to +960 at the same book. Both were players no preview mentioned. Being right is exactly why those prices moved — but it cost about half the edge on each.
