Reading this honestly

Every position was taken against our own model

All four bets were rated negative by the board on this same page. That is not a detail to bury: the rule is that a model-negative position gets argued in writing before it is placed, and these were. Two of them won. The two that lost were equally model-negative — so this week is one counter-instance, not proof the rule is wrong.

All the profit came from one name held twice

Wyndham Clark carried both winning tickets, and they settled together. Two bets on one player is one outcome wearing two stakes, and it was flagged as such before placement. It paid this time. Strip this single week out and the season’s outright betting is a coin flip.

The board found the golf; the card found the money somewhere else

The model had Cantlay, McIlroy and Gotterup rated — three of the actual top four — and had Morikawa 13th before he finished T4. It also ranked the winner 29th of 50. Scored afterwards on where each weighting placed the fifteen players who finished T12 or better, no version of the board beat chance, and the run with no course profile at all scored best.

Morikawa was the structural miss

A T4 in a 50-man no-cut field returns nothing on a win-only ticket. Our own rule says a mid-price name at a hard-scoring course takes the each-way ladder, not the straight win. That rule was not applied here, and it is the one part of the week that would have paid more with no better read at all.
